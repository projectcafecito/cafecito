# Third-Party Content Policy

**Effective date:** September 16, 2026

Project Cafecito helps users discover and analyze information from public-information and publicly available sources/outlets. This policy explains the boundary between third-party material, Cafecito-generated output, and downstream use.

## Content model

Cafecito Services may provide third-party source material; factual source metadata; source-provided expression such as titles, quotations, summaries, excerpts, or available content; Cafecito-generated attributes such as categories, regions, entities, sentiment, scores, and relationships; and Cafecito-published analysis such as Events, Signals, summaries, and editorials.

The presence of material in a Cafecito product does not determine or expand the legal rights attached to it.

## Ownership and permitted use

Third-Party Content remains owned by the applicable author, source, licensor, or other rights holder. Cafecito does not claim ownership of it. Public availability, attribution, or a source link does not itself grant a right to copy, display, distribute, archive, resell, create datasets from, place into permanent RAG storage, or use material for model training.

Unless Cafecito documentation or a written agreement expressly permits broader use, customers must use Third-Party Content only as reasonably necessary for internal evaluation, search, analysis, and use of the Services. Customers must preserve available attribution and canonical source links and obtain additional permission required for their intended downstream use.

## Source fidelity and generated output

Where available, Cafecito identifies the source, canonical URL, author, and publication date. Customers should preserve words such as “alleged,” “reported,” “charged,” or “according to” when they are material to meaning and should verify important information at the original source.

Generated attributes and Cafecito-published analysis may be incomplete, inaccurate, delayed, or affected by ambiguity. They do not represent endorsement by a referenced source and must not be presented as source-authored statements.

## Source controls and content requests

Authors, sources, rights holders, subjects, and users may request review of incorrect attribution; source corrections, retractions, or withdrawals; incorrect generated attributes or mistaken identity; source restrictions or opt-outs; privacy, safety, or doxxing concerns; alleged infringement; or licensing discussions.

Email [support@cafecito.tech](mailto:support@cafecito.tech) with the most relevant subject: **Content Rights Request**, **Publisher Opt-Out**, **Correction Request**, or **Copyright Notice**. Identify the affected canonical URL or Cafecito record and the requested action. Do not send credentials, unnecessary sensitive information, or complete copies of the disputed work.

## Review and outcomes

Cafecito may request information reasonably necessary to verify identity, authority, the affected material, and the basis for the request. Depending on urgency and risk, Cafecito may temporarily restrict material while reviewing it.

Outcomes may include correction, source refresh, attribution change, annotation, suppression, removal, source restriction, restoration, or no action. We keep records reasonably needed to apply decisions consistently, honor restrictions, prevent repeat issues, and meet legal obligations.
